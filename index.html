A note for your next meeting

Stop waiting
for late people.

Every minute you hold the meeting for stragglers, you punish the people who showed up on time — and teach everyone else that being late is fine.

The math is simple
10

×
5

=
50
minutes wasted — for what?

You burned nearly an hour of collective time to spare one person a mildly awkward entrance. That one person will be late again next week.

1

Waiting rewards the wrong behavior

If the meeting starts on time whether you're there or not, being late has a cost: you miss things. If the meeting waits for you, being late has no cost at all — in fact, it's efficient to show up late and skip the dead air. You've accidentally made punctuality the bad strategy.

2

It punishes everyone who showed up

The people sitting there staring at a loading spinner did nothing wrong. They planned ahead, they carved out the time, they're ready to go. Holding them hostage to accommodate someone else's schedule signals that their time matters less than the latecomer's comfort.

3

Starting on time is the norm that fixes itself

The first meeting you start exactly on time will feel rude. The second one, people will notice. By the third, everyone shows up on time — because the meeting actually starts when it says it does. Late arrivals catch up. The culture corrects itself.

The rule

Start the meeting at the scheduled time. Every time.

Not "once most people are on." Not "let's give it two more minutes." At the time on the calendar. Latecomers are welcome to join — you keep going. No recap. No pause. The meeting started when it was supposed to.
